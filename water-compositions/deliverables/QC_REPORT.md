## Approach

- **Tooling:** both projects were scaffolded with the Remotion CLI (`npx create-video --blank`, Remotion 4.0.539).
- **Technology:** WebGL 2 fragment shaders (GLSL ES 3.00), drawn by a small reusable `ShaderCanvas` component. Both references are procedural water textures (refraction caustics and specular ripple lines), and a per-pixel shader is the natural fit for that.
  - There are no image or video assets; everything is generated by the shaders.
  - Every frame is a pure function of `frame / 30`, so renders are deterministic.
- **Resolution:** compositions are authored at 3840×2160. The 1080p deliverables are rendered from those 4K compositions with `--scale=0.5`; the shader is still evaluated at 4K and then downsampled (2×2 supersampling).
- **Composition 1, Caustic Pool:**
  - Animated Voronoi caustic network with three-stage domain warping (slow undulation, border smear, fast shimmer).
  - Marbled folds, speckles, and a persistent sun-glow gradient measured from the reference.
  - **V2 (alternative colour palette):** the same simulation through an emerald, mint and pale-gold colour ramp. A palette change suits this clip best: its identity is the caustic pattern seen from above, and a camera move would hide that.
- **Composition 2, Ripple Surface:**
  - Point-source ripple trains (centres just off-frame) with chirped spacing.
  - Cross-train phase coupling, which produces kinks instead of interference grids.
  - A per-place field that decides which crests are bold and which are soft.
  - Fast surface jitter, short-lived zig-zag kinks, crest glints, mottling and a vignette.
  - **V2 (alternative camera angle and movement):** the identical water seen from a low-angle perspective camera, about 24° below the horizon, with a slow forward dolly and a pan of about 30°. Distance haze and wavelength band-limiting keep the far field from aliasing. The reference is a flat top-down surface study, so a grazing camera move changes it most clearly while keeping the grey palette and the ripple concept intact.

**Duration note:** both references are 29.97 fps NTSC files (480 frames = 16.016 s, 540 frames = 18.018 s). At the required true 30 fps, a fractional frame count is impossible. Each video therefore keeps the reference's exact frame count, so every reference frame maps one-to-one to an output frame. This gives 16.000 s and 18.000 s, a difference of 16–18 ms from the 29.97 fps timebase. 480 and 540 frames are also the closest possible lengths at 30 fps.

**Audio note:** the second reference contains an audio track, but it is pure digital silence (−91 dB, constant). The first reference has no audio. No audio was produced, so there are no audio issues to check.

## Independent verification agent

A separate agent compared each render against its reference using side-by-side sheets, sequences of consecutive frames, 100% crops and its own measurements. Its findings drove several revisions before delivery.

**Composition 1, V1:**
- First render: 7/10. Its cells were too large and its lines looked like lightning.
- Revised render: **8/10, PASS.**
  - Cell scale, colour, lighting gradient and motion speed now match the reference.
  - Remaining minor notes: the diagonal shear is slightly strong, and there is a little less fine shimmer than in the reference.

**Composition 1, V2:** **PASS.**
- The emerald palette is unmistakably different, and the structure, motion and lighting are identical to V1.
- There is no banding or clipping.

**Composition 2, V1:** the score went 2 → 5 → 6.5 → 6.5 → 6 → **7/10, PASS** over five redesigns. These fixed, in turn:
- the interference lattice
- line density
- the arc geometry
- rigid motion
- the crest balance
- washed-out windows

Remaining non-blocking notes:
- The arcs are somewhat more regular than the reference.
- The water between crests has a slightly cellular mottle.
- There is no single large blown-out flare like the reference's at about 8 s.

**Composition 2, V2:** **PASS.**
- It is clearly distinct from V1 and the reference, keeps the concept, and the camera moves smoothly with no judder.
- Horizon band-limiting works: there is no shimmer.
- Notes:
  - The last third (about 12–18 s) is less contrasty than the first half.
  - The horizon shows faint perspective striping at 100% zoom.

All four videos were also checked for visual artifacts at 100% crop. The agent found no aliasing, banding, seams or glitches, and the iStock watermark from the references is not reproduced anywhere.

## How to render

These commands are also in each project's `README.md`.

```console
npm ci
npx remotion render <CompositionId> out/<name>_1080p.mp4 --scale=0.5   # 1080p deliverable
npx remotion render <CompositionId> out/<name>_4K.mp4                  # native 4K master
```

- **CPU vs GPU:** `remotion.config.ts` uses the SwiftShader WebGL backend (`swangle`), which works on any machine. Expect about 2–3 s per 4K frame on 4 CPU cores. On a machine with a GPU, add `--gl=angle` to render much faster.
- **Encoding settings:** H.264, CRF 16, x264 preset `slow`, yuv420p.
